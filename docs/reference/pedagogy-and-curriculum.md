## PART A — Pedagogy for a rigorous-but-digestible math platform

### A1. Evidence-based techniques and how to implement each as a web component

**1. Worked examples and faded worked examples.** Cognitive load research shows that novices learn a procedure better from studying fully worked solutions than from solving problems, and that the advantage reverses as expertise grows; "fading" (removing solution steps one at a time, back-to-front, until the learner solves alone) manages that transition and improves near transfer in math and physics ([Renkl & Atkinson, worked-example effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect); [Renkl, Atkinson et al., structuring the transition from example study to problem solving](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/structuring-the-transition-from-example-study-to-problem-solving--2)). Self-explanation prompts ("why is this step allowed?") multiply the effect.
*Implementation:* a `<DerivationExample>` component rendering a step list where each step has `{statement, justification, status: shown | faded}`. Generate a 4-example sequence per derivation: fully worked, last step blank, last two blank, all blank. A faded step becomes an input (math-expression input with CAS-lite equivalence via `mathjs`/`compute-engine`, or a pick-from-3-candidates widget when free-form checking is too hard). Each step has a "Why?" toggle for the self-explanation.

**2. Retrieval practice (testing effect).** Actively recalling from memory beats re-reading; the effect is largest when retrieval is frequent, low-stakes, and followed by feedback ([Dunlosky et al. summary via Learning Scientists](https://learningscientists.org/blog/2021/7/1-1); [Rohrer / IES interleaved mathematics practice](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/interleaved-mathematics-practice)). In math the robustness of pure retrieval is somewhat weaker than in verbal domains ([Murray, Horner & Göbel meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10648-020-09516-2)), so pair it with worked examples rather than replacing them.
*Implementation:* every lesson section ends with a `<CheckYourself>` (1–3 items, inline, no page navigation, immediate feedback with the relevant derivation step linked). A "Daily 5" retrieval widget on the dashboard draws from all units mastered so far.

**3. Spaced repetition: SM-2 vs FSRS.** SM-2 (1987) keeps one "ease factor" per card and multiplies the interval by it; FSRS (2022–24) models each card with Difficulty, Stability (days until recall probability falls to 90%) and Retrievability, is trained on ~700M Anki reviews, and in benchmarks reduces reviews 15–40% at equal retention with log-loss 0.327 vs 0.354 for SM-2 ([FSRS vs SM-2 benchmark summary](https://www.diane.app/en/guides/fsrs-vs-sm2); [algorithm wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/wiki/The-Algorithm)). FSRS has MIT-licensed TypeScript, Rust and Python implementations.
*Implementation:* use [`ts-fsrs`](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/ts-fsrs) with default parameters and desired retention 0.9; store per-card `{due, stability, difficulty, reps, lapses, state}` per user; expose the four-button rating (Again/Hard/Good/Easy). SM-2 is acceptable as a fallback (30 lines of code) but FSRS also gives you a probability-of-recall number per concept, which is exactly what the concept-graph "mastery glow" should be driven by.

**4. Interleaving.** Mixing problem types from different units (instead of blocked practice) forces learners to choose the method, not just execute it; in a randomized classroom trial interleaved math practice raised unannounced-test scores from 38% to 61% ([Rohrer, Dedrick & Stershic, IES efficacy study](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/efficacy-study-interleaved-mathematics-practice)). It is especially valuable for look-alike concepts (ridge vs lasso, LDA vs logistic regression, forward vs reverse KL).
*Implementation:* the practice queue samples by concept-graph *neighborhood* (siblings of the current node), never only the current lesson; "boss quizzes" (A4) are interleaved by construction; tag each item with `discriminates: [conceptA, conceptB]` so the sampler can deliberately pair confusables.

**5. Dual coding / multimedia principles.** Verbal and visual channels are separate and additive; Mayer's principles that matter most for derivations are *segmenting* (one step at a time, learner-paced), *signaling* (color/highlight the term that is changing), *spatial and temporal contiguity* (put the picture next to the equation it explains), and *coherence* (no decorative extras) ([Mayer's 12 principles](https://services.dartmouth.edu/TDClient/1806/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=171655); [Mayer & Moreno 2002](https://www.sfu.ca/~jcnesbit/EDUC220/ThinkPaper/MayerMoreno2002.pdf)).
*Implementation:* a `<SyncedFigure>` pattern: an equation on the left and a plot on the right share a color map (the $\theta^\top x$ term is blue in the equation and the blue line in the plot). Hovering any symbol in KaTeX output (wrap symbols in `\htmlClass{sym-theta}{\theta}`) highlights its visual counterpart. Every derivation step may carry an optional `figureState` so stepping forward updates the figure.

**6. Cognitive load theory for multi-step derivations.** Intrinsic load (element interactivity) is fixed by the math; extraneous load is what the design controls. For a 12-step MLE derivation the losses come from split attention (the justification is far from the step), redundancy, and lack of chunking. Remedies: integrate justifications inline, group steps into 3–4 named chunks ("write the likelihood", "take logs", "differentiate", "solve"), and show the goal state first ("we will arrive at $\hat\theta = \#H/(\#H + \#T)$").
*Implementation:* the derivation component has a mandatory `goal` banner, `chunks[]` with headings, a per-step `justification` shown as a right-margin note on wide screens and as an inline expandable on phones, and a "collapse to summary" mode that shows only chunk headings and final result.

**7. Explorable explanations.** Bret Victor's 2011 essay argues for "active readers" who can test the author's claims by manipulating them ([Victor](https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/)); Nicky Case's practical rules are "do, then show, then tell" — use interactivity only where it beats text, and structure the piece along an interest curve that starts with a hook requiring little prior knowledge ([Case](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations)).
*Implementation:* a small set of reusable "explorables" rather than one per lesson: parameter sliders on a model + loss surface; a draggable data point; a "turn the knob $\lambda$" regularization path; a threshold slider on an ROC curve; an EM animation. Each explorable has a "challenge" line ("find an $\eta$ that diverges") so manipulation has a goal.

**Canonical sequence to borrow:** motivation/hook → definition (boxed) → intuition (picture or 1-D case) → derivation (chunked, step-reveal) → visualization (explorable) → worked example (clinical) → check-your-understanding → connection to homework/code companion → summary card (which becomes the flashcards).

### A3. Proposed canonical lesson anatomy

Each lecture becomes 2–5 **units**; each unit is one concept-graph node and uses this template:

1. **Header strip** — unit title, estimated time, prerequisite chips (link to sticky-notes), and the four-component frame: *Data · Model class · Objective · Optimizer* with this unit's instance filled in.
2. **Hook (≤ 120 words + 1 explorable or figure)** — a clinical question from Part C ("Which dose of the new antihypertensive should the Phase II trial use?").
3. **Definitions** — boxed, numbered `Def U.1`, with the course symbol and an "also written as" line.
4. **Intuition** — the 1-D or 2-point case, drawn; dual-coded with the formula.
5. **Derivation(s)** — one `<Derivation>` per named result (e.g. "Derivation 3.2: normal equations"). Goal banner; chunks; step-reveal; justification notes; final result boxed and auto-registered as a flashcard.
6. **Visualization / explorable** — the same figure state the derivation ended on, now manipulable.
7. **Worked example (clinical)** — numbers from the synthetic dataset; then a faded version.
8. **Check your understanding** — 2–4 inline items (A4 types), graded instantly.
9. **Pitfalls** — the misconception list for the topic (Part B), each phrased as a false statement with the fix.
10. **Connections** — links to the homework problem, the code companion notebook cell, the prerequisite sticky-notes, and the next unit.
11. **Summary card** — definitions + results in one screen; this is the source of the unit's flashcards.

**Rules for which math treatment to use:**

| Use | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Step-reveal derivation** (default) | The result is *examinable*, is 4–15 steps, and every step uses only tools from prerequisites or earlier units | Normal equations; Bernoulli MLE → cross-entropy; ridge closed form; GDA posterior is a sigmoid; backprop for a 2-layer MLP |
| **Collapsed proof** (shown in full but folded; summary + "key idea" visible) | The result matters but the proof is > 15 steps, needs a tool outside the course (measure theory, convex analysis), or is "nice to know" per the syllabus | GD convergence rate for $L$-smooth convex functions; universal approximation; k-means++ $O(\log k)$ guarantee; Jensen's inequality proof |
| **Sticky-note** (hover/side-panel refresher, ≤ 150 words + 1 figure) | A prerequisite fact used *inside* a step: chain rule, $\nabla_\theta \theta^\top A \theta = 2A\theta$, Bayes' rule, trace trick, $\log$ turns products into sums | Appears inline on the step that uses it |
| **"Proof sketch" inline** | A one-line argument suffices and a full proof would be padding | "KL ≥ 0 by Jensen on $-\log$" with a link to the collapsed proof |

**Step-reveal component contract (for engineers):** `steps: [{tex, justificationTex?, stickyNoteId?, figureState?, fadeable: boolean}]`, `chunks: [{title, stepRange}]`, `goalTex`, `resultTex`, `flashcardIds`. Keyboard: →/space reveals, ← hides, `a` reveals all. Every step is addressable by URL fragment so quizzes and homework hints can deep-link to "step 7 of Derivation 5.1".

### A4. Quiz and flashcard design for math; tasteful gamification

**Question types that work for derivations and models (each with an auto-grader):**

1. **Multiple choice with misconception-built distractors.** Each distractor encodes a documented error (e.g. "KL is symmetric", "ridge sets coefficients to exactly zero", "accuracy is the right metric with 2% prevalence"). Tag distractors with `misconceptionId` so the pitfalls section can show the learner's *own* errors.
2. **Numeric with tolerance.** "With $\#H = 12$, $\#T = 28$, $\hat\theta_{\text{MLE}} = $ ___" (tol ±0.005). Use a seeded generator so numbers vary per attempt (parameters drawn from a small grid; answers computed server-side or in-browser).
3. **"Which step is wrong?"** Erroneous worked examples attributed to a fictional student, where the learner must locate, explain and correct the error; shown to deepen understanding versus solving isomorphic problems ([McLaren & Adams, AdaptErrEx](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/adapterrex-exploring-learning-benefits-erroneous-examples-and-their-dynamic-adaptations-within); [Tsovaltzi et al. 2009](https://www-live.dfki.de/fileadmin/user_upload/import/4653_Tsovaltzietal-ErroneousExamples-ECTEL-2009.pdf)). Build them from the step-reveal data: clone a derivation, corrupt one step (drop a $-$ sign, forget the chain rule factor $\sigma(1-\sigma)$, swap $p$ and $q$ in KL), and ask for the step index plus the correction from 3 choices.
4. **Match term ↔ formula / symbol ↔ meaning.** Drag-and-drop pairs ("$\psi_{jk}$" ↔ "probability word $j$ appears in class $k$"). Useful for the overloaded $\phi$.
5. **Parameter-estimation mini-problems.** A 6-row table of synthetic patients; compute $\mu_1$, $\phi_1$, or a Gini impurity; graded numerically.
6. **Order the steps.** Shuffled derivation steps to be sorted (a cheap retrieval exercise with high diagnostic value).
7. **Predict-then-observe (explorable-bound).** "If $\lambda \to \infty$, where do ridge coefficients go?" answer, then move the slider.
8. **Code-trace.** "Which line of this 10-line GD loop implements $\theta^{k+1} = \theta^k - \eta \nabla$?" tying to the code companions.

**Flashcards for formulas.** Wozniak's rules (atomic cards, minimal context, understand first) still hold, and the community caveat is that plain cloze on formulas produces "deck learning" ([Anki best-practice discussions](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7Q7DPSk4iGFJd8DRk/an-opinionated-guide-to-using-anki-correctly)). So use three card shapes per result:
- *Cloze-one-term*: "$\theta^* = (X^\top X + \text{\{\{c1::}}\lambda I\text{\}\}})^{-1} X^\top y$ — what guarantees invertibility?" (deletes the term that carries the idea, not a random symbol).
- *Statement → formula* with a "say it in words" back side ("ridge adds $\lambda$ to every eigenvalue of $X^\top X$").
- *Formula → when/why* ("When is this the MLE?" → "Gaussian noise with equal variance").
Cards carry `conceptId` and `derivationStep` links; FSRS schedules them; the concept node's glow is the min retrievability of its cards.

**Gamification that stays tasteful.** Khan Academy's five-level mastery ladder (Not started / Attempted / Familiar / Proficient / Mastered) is tied to demonstrated competence and was associated with +0.12 to +0.22 SD math gains in a large RCT ([PNAS 2026 summary](https://www.engageli.com/blog/gamification-in-education)); Duolingo's streak mechanic is the single strongest retention driver in consumer edtech ([Duolingo gamification case](https://www.strivecloud.io/blog/blog-gamification-examples-boost-user-retention-duolingo)). Recommended set:
- **Mastery per concept node** (5 levels), promoted only by *spaced* correct retrievals, demoted by lapses (FSRS state drives it). Nodes light up in the concept graph; edges light when both endpoints are Proficient.
- **XP** for completed derivations, correct checks, and reviews — with diminishing returns on re-doing the same item (prevents farming).
- **Streaks** counted on *review days*, with a weekly "streak freeze" so it does not become punitive before exams.
- **Boss quiz per lecture**: 10–12 interleaved items spanning all units of the lecture plus two from earlier lectures; passing (≥ 80%) unlocks the "lecture crest" and marks the lecture node gold. Allow retakes with fresh seeds.
- **Derivation speed-run** (optional): re-order shuffled steps of a derivation you already mastered, timed — fun, low stakes.
- Avoid leaderboards in a graduate course cohort; use a private "you vs your past self" chart instead.

### Covered so far (L1–L10)

**(1) Supervised learning setup and empirical risk minimization.**
*Notation:* $\mathcal{D}$, $x^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $y^{(i)}$, $\mathcal{M} = \{f_\theta\}$, loss $\mathrm{loss}(\hat y, y)$, empirical risk $\mathrm{Loss}(\theta)$ (slides) a.k.a. $\hat R_n(\theta) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \mathrm{loss}(f_\theta(x^{(i)}), y^{(i)})$, true risk $R(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(X,Y) \sim P}[\mathrm{loss}(f_\theta(X), Y)]$. *Definitions:* i.i.d. assumption; data-generating distribution $P$; the four components (data, model class, objective, optimizer); regression vs classification; features vs attributes; one-hot encoding of categoricals; train/test split. *Derive:* (a) that $\hat R_n(\theta)$ is an unbiased estimator of $R(\theta)$ for *fixed* $\theta$ (linearity of expectation) and why that fails for $\hat\theta$ chosen by minimizing (selection bias → overfitting; preview of L8); (b) the Bayes-optimal predictor for squared loss is $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid X]$ (expand $\mathbb{E}[(Y - f(X))^2]$, add and subtract $\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]$, cross term vanishes). *Visualizations:* scatter of BMI vs outcome with candidate lines and their loss; a "population vs sample" animation (resampling the dataset). *Pitfalls:* confusing loss (per example) with risk (expectation); believing low training loss implies low risk; treating zip codes as ordinal. *Refs:* [AML Lecture 2](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture2-supervised-learning.html); [d2l 3.1](https://d2l.ai/chapter_linear-regression/linear-regression.html), [d2l 3.6 Generalization](https://d2l.ai/chapter_linear-regression/generalization.html); ESL §2.4; PML1 ch. 1, §4.

**(2) Linear regression.**
*Notation:* $f_\theta(x) = \theta^\top x$ with $x_0 = 1$; design matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times (d+1)}$, $y \in \mathbb{R}^n$; squared loss $\ell(\theta) = \|X\theta - y\|_2^2$ (slides omit $\frac{1}{2n}$; flag it). *Definitions:* residual, least squares, polynomial features via $\phi$. *Derive:* (a) **Normal equations**: $\ell(\theta) = \theta^\top X^\top X \theta - 2y^\top X \theta + y^\top y$; use $\nabla_\theta \theta^\top A \theta = 2A\theta$ (sticky), $\nabla_\theta b^\top \theta = b$; set $\nabla = 0$ → $X^\top X \theta = X^\top y$ → $\theta^* = (X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top y$ when $X$ has full column rank; Hessian $2X^\top X \succeq 0$ confirms a minimum. (b) **Geometric/projection view**: $X\theta^*$ is the orthogonal projection of $y$ onto $\mathrm{col}(X)$; residual $y - X\theta^* \perp \mathrm{col}(X)$ ⇔ $X^\top (y - X\theta^*) = 0$, the same equations; hat matrix $H = X(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top$ is symmetric idempotent. (c) **Probabilistic/MLE view**: $y = \theta^\top x + \epsilon$, $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ ⇒ $P_\theta(y \mid x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma} \exp(-\frac{(y - \theta^\top x)^2}{2\sigma^2})$; log-likelihood $= -\frac{n}{2}\log(2\pi\sigma^2) - \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_i (y^{(i)} - \theta^\top x^{(i)})^2$; maximizing over $\theta$ ⇔ least squares (the slide's "$c_1 \cdot (\cdot)^2 + c_2$"). Also derive $\hat\sigma^2_{\text{MLE}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum r_i^2$. *Visualizations:* 1-D fit with residual segments; 3-D column-space projection picture; loss surface over $(\theta_0, \theta_1)$ with contours. *Pitfalls:* $X^\top X$ singular with collinear or $d > n$ features (ties into ridge); interpreting coefficients causally; forgetting the intercept; thinking MLE needs $\sigma$ known. *Refs:* [AML Lecture 3](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture3-linear-regression.html); CS229 §1.1–1.3; MML ch. 9; ESL §3.2; [d2l 3.1](https://d2l.ai/chapter_linear-regression/linear-regression.html).

**(3) Gradient descent.**
*Notation:* $\theta^{k+1} = \theta^k - \eta^k \nabla_\theta \mathrm{Loss}(\theta^k)$; batch (full sum), SGD (one example $i_k$), mini-batch (set $B_k$, $|B_k| = b$); condition number $\kappa = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$ of the Hessian. *Definitions:* gradient as direction of steepest ascent (slides: $\Delta y = \langle \nabla y, \Delta X \rangle$ maximized when aligned); Hessian; convex function ($f(\lambda x + (1-\lambda)y) \le \lambda f(x) + (1-\lambda)f(y)$, equivalently $\nabla^2 f \succeq 0$); $L$-smooth ($\|\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\| \le L\|x - y\|$); $\mu$-strongly convex. *Derive:* (a) **Descent lemma**: $L$-smooth ⇒ $f(y) \le f(x) + \nabla f(x)^\top (y - x) + \frac{L}{2}\|y - x\|^2$; plug $y = x - \eta \nabla f(x)$ ⇒ $f(x^{k+1}) \le f(x^k) - \eta(1 - \frac{L\eta}{2})\|\nabla f(x^k)\|^2$, so any $\eta < 2/L$ decreases $f$ (this *is* the slides' "$\eta < 2\eta_{\text{opt}}$" with $\eta_{\text{opt}} = 1/L$ for a quadratic). (b) **Rate for convex + $L$-smooth with $\eta = 1/L$**: $f(x^k) - f^* \le \frac{L\|x^0 - x^*\|^2}{2k}$ (telescoping sum of descent-lemma + convexity inequalities; collapsed proof). (c) **Linear rate for strongly convex**: $\|x^k - x^*\|^2 \le (1 - \mu/L)^k\|x^0 - x^*\|^2$ — exhibits $\kappa$. (d) **1-D quadratic exact analysis** $f(\theta) = \frac{a}{2}\theta^2$: $\theta^{k+1} = (1 - \eta a)\theta^k$; converges iff $|1 - \eta a| < 1$ ⇔ $0 < \eta < 2/a$; oscillates for $1/a < \eta < 2/a$; the multi-dimensional case diagonalizes to this per eigen-direction (this reproduces the L5 slide sequence exactly). (e) **SGD is unbiased**: $\mathbb{E}_{i}[\nabla \mathrm{loss}_i(\theta)] = \nabla \mathrm{Loss}(\theta)$ for uniform $i$ (one line); variance $\propto 1/b$ for mini-batches. *Visualizations:* contour plot with GD path for $\eta$ small / optimal / oscillating / diverging; elongated ellipses to show $\kappa$; SGD path jitter vs batch; loss-vs-iteration curves. *Pitfalls:* "GD always converges"; "a smaller $\eta$ is always safer" (too slow); feature scaling changes $\kappa$; stopping on gradient norm vs loss change; thinking SGD noise is only harmful. *Refs:* [AML Lecture 3/4 (optimization sections)](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture3-linear-regression.html); [d2l 12.2 Convexity](https://d2l.ai/chapter_optimization/convexity.html), [12.3 GD](https://d2l.ai/chapter_optimization/gd.html), [12.4 SGD](https://d2l.ai/chapter_optimization/sgd.html), [12.5 Minibatch](https://d2l.ai/chapter_optimization/minibatch-sgd.html); MML ch. 7; [Distill momentum article](https://distill.pub/2017/momentum/) for the quadratic analysis.

**(4) Logistic regression and classification.**
*Notation:* $y \in \{0, 1\}$; $\sigma(z) = (1 + e^{-z})^{-1}$; $f_\theta(x) = \sigma(\theta^\top x) = P_\theta(y = 1 \mid x)$; log-odds $\log \frac{p}{1-p} = \theta^\top x$; cross-entropy loss $\ell(\theta) = -\sum_i [y^{(i)}\log \sigma(\theta^\top x^{(i)}) + (1 - y^{(i)})\log(1 - \sigma(\theta^\top x^{(i)}))]$. *Definitions:* decision boundary $\{x : \theta^\top x = 0\}$ is linear; linear separability; softmax for $K$ classes $P(y = k \mid x) = \frac{e^{\theta_k^\top x}}{\sum_j e^{\theta_j^\top x}}$. *Derive:* (a) sigmoid from log-odds (solve $\log\frac{p}{1-p} = z$ for $p$); (b) $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z))$; (c) **Bernoulli likelihood → cross-entropy**: $P_\theta(y \mid x) = \sigma^y(1 - \sigma)^{1-y}$ (compact form; note the slides write the equivalent $y\sigma + (1-y)(1-\sigma)$ *inside* the product — the platform should show both and explain they agree for $y \in \{0,1\}$), product over $i$, log, negate; (d) **gradient** $\nabla_\theta \ell = \sum_i (\sigma(\theta^\top x^{(i)}) - y^{(i)})\, x^{(i)}$ — same shape as linear regression's gradient (GLM foreshadowing); (e) **no closed form** (the gradient equation is transcendental in $\theta$) and (f) **convexity**: Hessian $= \sum_i \sigma_i(1 - \sigma_i)\, x^{(i)} x^{(i)\top} \succeq 0$; (g) why squared loss on $\sigma(\theta^\top x)$ is non-convex (L7 "L2 or KL?" slide) — show the 1-D plot. *Visualizations:* 1-D sigmoid fit over 0/1 dots (the slides' red/blue); 2-D decision boundary; loss landscape CE vs squared for a 1-D example; separable data → $\|\theta\| \to \infty$ animation. *Pitfalls:* outputs are probabilities only if calibrated; "logistic regression is a regression"; perfect separation makes MLE diverge (needs regularization); threshold 0.5 is not sacred (→ L7). *Refs:* [AML Lecture 4](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture4-classification.html); CS229 §2.1–2.3, ch. 3 (GLMs); [d2l 4.1 Softmax](https://d2l.ai/chapter_linear-classification/softmax-regression.html); PML1 ch. 10; [MLU-Explain logistic regression](https://mlu-explain.github.io/logistic-regression/).

**(5) MLE in general and its relation to KL / cross-entropy.**
*Notation:* $\mathcal{L}(\theta) = P_\theta(\mathcal{D}) = \prod_i P_\theta(x^{(i)})$, $\hat\theta_{\text{MLE}} = \arg\max \log \mathcal{L}(\theta)$; data distribution $p_{\text{data}}$, model $q_\theta$; cross-entropy $H(p, q) = -\mathbb{E}_p[\log q]$. *Derive:* (a) coin flip: $\log P = \#H \log\theta + \#T \log(1-\theta)$, derivative zero ⇒ $\hat\theta = \#H/(\#H + \#T)$, check second derivative; (b) Gaussian mean/variance MLE; (c) **MLE = minimizing KL to the empirical distribution**: $\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \log q_\theta(x^{(i)}) = \mathbb{E}_{\hat p}[\log q_\theta] = -H(\hat p, q_\theta) = -H(\hat p) - D(\hat p \,\|\, q_\theta)$, and $H(\hat p)$ is constant in $\theta$ (this is the L6 slide "$\arg\min_\theta D(p\|q) = \arg\min_\theta \sum -\log q_\theta$"); conditional version with $p(y \mid x)$; (d) invariance of MLE under reparametrization (one line); (e) consistency statement (collapsed, no proof). *Visualizations:* likelihood curve for the coin; "move the Gaussian until the data are most probable" slider; KL-as-gap figure. *Pitfalls:* likelihood is not a probability of $\theta$; MLE can overfit (variance MLE biased by $\frac{n-1}{n}$); maximizing the *joint* vs the *conditional* likelihood (the slides emphasize the $\prod P(X_i)$ factor dropping out). *Refs:* [AML Lecture 4 (MLE section)](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture4-classification.html); CS229 §1.3, §2.1; [d2l 22.7 Maximum Likelihood](https://d2l.ai/chapter_appendix-mathematics-for-deep-learning/maximum-likelihood.html); MML §8.3; PML1 §4.2–4.3.

**(6) Classification evaluation.**
*Notation:* TP/FP/TN/FN; accuracy; precision $= \frac{TP}{TP + FP}$; recall/sensitivity/TPR $= \frac{TP}{TP + FN}$; specificity/TNR; FPR $= 1 -$ TNR; $F_1 = \frac{2PR}{P + R}$ (harmonic mean); macro vs micro averaging (L7 shows micro: $\sum_k TP_k / \sum_k (TP_k + FP_k)$); ROC curve (FPR, TPR) over thresholds $t$; AUC; PR curve; calibration curve / reliability diagram, Brier score, ECE. *Derive:* (a) $F_1$ as harmonic mean and why it penalizes imbalance between P and R; (b) **AUC = $P(\text{score}_{\text{pos}} > \text{score}_{\text{neg}})$** (probabilistic interpretation via the Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney statistic; collapsed); (c) the threshold that maximizes expected utility given costs $c_{FP}, c_{FN}$ and prevalence $\pi$: predict positive iff $P(y = 1 \mid x) > \frac{c_{FP}}{c_{FP} + c_{FN}}$ (Bayes decision theory, 5 steps); (d) that accuracy of the all-negative classifier equals $1 - \pi$ (why 98% accuracy can be useless); (e) PR curve's baseline is $\pi$ while ROC's chance line is the diagonal. *Visualizations:* a draggable threshold on two overlapping score histograms that simultaneously updates the confusion matrix, the point on the ROC and the point on the PR curve (the single most valuable explorable in the course); reliability diagram. *Pitfalls:* optimizing accuracy under imbalance; ROC looks fine while PR is terrible on rare events; "AUC 0.9 means 90% of predictions are right"; calibration ≠ discrimination; computing metrics on the training set. *Refs:* [MLU-Explain precision/recall](https://mlu-explain.github.io/precision-recall/) and [ROC/AUC](https://mlu-explain.github.io/roc-auc/); PML1 §5.1; [d2l 4.6](https://d2l.ai/chapter_linear-classification/generalization-classification.html); ESL §9.2.5 (ROC in context).

**(7) Divergences.**
*Notation:* $D(p \| q) = \sum_x p(x)\log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}$ (slides); entropy $H(p)$; cross-entropy $H(p, q) = H(p) + D(p\|q)$; Jensen–Shannon $\mathrm{JS}(p, q) = \frac{1}{2}D(p\|m) + \frac{1}{2}D(q\|m)$, $m = \frac{p+q}{2}$; the slides' "L2 divergence" $\|y - f_\theta(x)\|^2$ vs "KL divergence" as per-sample losses. *Derive:* (a) **Gibbs' inequality** $D(p\|q) \ge 0$ with equality iff $p = q$, via Jensen on $-\log$ (or $\log t \le t - 1$); (b) asymmetry by explicit 2-point counterexample; (c) **forward KL $D(p\|q)$ is mean-seeking / mass-covering, reverse KL $D(q\|p)$ is mode-seeking**: take $p$ a bimodal mixture and $q$ a single Gaussian; show where each is infinite (forward: wherever $p > 0, q = 0$; reverse: wherever $q > 0, p = 0$); (d) binary cross-entropy is KL between Bernoulli($y$) and Bernoulli($\sigma(\theta^\top x)$) up to the constant $H(\text{Bern}(y)) = 0$ for hard labels; (e) the L7 "desiderata" (steep far from optimum, smooth near it) with the plot of CE vs L2 against a sigmoid output. *Visualizations:* two-slider PMFs with live $D(p\|q)$ and $D(q\|p)$; Gaussian-fit-to-bimodal animation for forward vs reverse. *Pitfalls:* calling KL a distance; forgetting the support condition (absolute continuity); mixing natural log vs bits; thinking cross-entropy minimization and MLE are different things. *Refs:* [d2l 22.11 Information Theory](https://d2l.ai/chapter_appendix-mathematics-for-deep-learning/information-theory.html); MML §6.4 (brief), PML1 §6.2; PRML §1.6.

**(8) Regularization.**
*Notation:* $\ell_\lambda(\theta) = \sum_i \mathrm{Div}(y^{(i)}, f_\theta(x^{(i)})) + \lambda R(\theta)$; ridge $R = \|\theta\|_2^2$, lasso $R = \|\theta\|_1$, elastic net $\alpha\|\theta\|_1 + (1 - \alpha)\|\theta\|_2^2$; constrained form $\min \ell$ s.t. $\|\theta\| \le \lambda'$ (slides). *Derive:* (a) **ridge closed form** $\theta^* = (X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y$ (same calculus as (2) plus $\nabla \lambda\|\theta\|^2 = 2\lambda\theta$), and why $X^\top X + \lambda I \succ 0$ always (eigenvalues shift by $\lambda$); (b) in the eigenbasis of $X^\top X = V\Lambda V^\top$, ridge shrinks component $j$ by $\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_j + \lambda}$ (connects to condition number); (c) **lasso sparsity**: 1-D orthonormal case gives soft-thresholding $\hat\theta_j = \mathrm{sign}(\theta^{\text{OLS}}_j)(|\theta^{\text{OLS}}_j| - \lambda)_+$ via subgradients — the only derivation in the course that needs subgradients (sticky-note); plus the geometric diamond-vs-circle picture; (d) **MAP interpretation**: $\arg\max_\theta \log p(\mathcal{D} \mid \theta) + \log p(\theta)$; Gaussian prior $\theta \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^2 I)$ gives $\lambda = \sigma^2/\tau^2$ ridge; Laplace prior gives lasso; (e) equivalence of penalized and constrained forms via Lagrangian (collapsed). *Visualizations:* the ESL contour + constraint-region figure; coefficient paths vs $\lambda$ (the L7 lasso-on-diabetes figure); bias–variance vs $\lambda$ curve. *Pitfalls:* regularizing the intercept; not standardizing features before lasso/ridge; "lasso always picks the right features" (correlated features); choosing $\lambda$ on the test set. *Refs:* [AML Lecture 5](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture5-regularization.html); ESL §3.4; CS229 ch. 9 (regularization & model selection); [d2l 3.7 Weight Decay](https://d2l.ai/chapter_linear-regression/weight-decay.html); PML1 §11.3–11.4; MML §9.2.3 (MAP).

**(9) Bias–variance, overfitting, model selection, cross-validation.**
*Notation:* $y = f(x) + \epsilon$, $\mathbb{E}\epsilon = 0$, $\mathrm{Var}\,\epsilon = \sigma^2$; estimator $\hat f_{\mathcal{D}}$ trained on random $\mathcal{D}$; expected test error at $x_0$. *Derive:* **the full decomposition** $\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D},\epsilon}[(y_0 - \hat f_{\mathcal{D}}(x_0))^2] = \sigma^2 + (\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\hat f(x_0) - f(x_0))^2 + \mathrm{Var}_{\mathcal{D}}(\hat f(x_0))$ — steps: add/subtract $f(x_0)$ (noise term separates because $\epsilon \perp \mathcal{D}$), then add/subtract $\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\hat f(x_0)$ (cross term vanishes because the deviation has zero mean). Then: training error is a biased-low estimate of risk (selection); holdout error is unbiased for the trained model; **k-fold CV** estimates risk of the *procedure*, with the $k$-vs-bias-vs-variance trade-off (LOOCV low bias/high variance and $n$ fits); nested CV for hyperparameters; validation set vs test set; grid/random search. *Visualizations:* polynomial degree sweep with train/val curves; the "many resampled fits" fan plot showing bias vs variance directly; k-fold diagram; double-descent teaser (MLU-Explain). *Pitfalls:* tuning on the test set; leakage through preprocessing fitted on all data (standardize inside the fold); CV with temporally or patient-level dependent rows (use grouped folds — a clinical point); "more data always fixes bias". *Refs:* ESL §7.2–7.3, §7.10; [MLU-Explain bias–variance](https://mlu-explain.github.io/bias-variance/), [cross-validation](https://mlu-explain.github.io/cross-validation/), [train/test/validation](https://mlu-explain.github.io/train-test-validation/); CS229 ch. 8–9; PML1 §4.5, §5.4; [d2l 3.6](https://d2l.ai/chapter_linear-regression/generalization.html).

**(10) Generative vs discriminative models; Bayes optimal classifier.**
*Notation:* discriminative $p_\theta(y \mid x)$; generative $p_\theta(x \mid y)$ and $p_\theta(y)$, joint $p_\theta(x, y)$; posterior $p(y \mid x) = \frac{p(x \mid y)p(y)}{\sum_{y'} p(x \mid y')p(y')}$; Bayes classifier $h^*(x) = \arg\max_y p(y \mid x)$. *Derive:* (a) the Bayes classifier minimizes 0-1 risk (pointwise argument: for each $x$, error $= 1 - p(h(x) \mid x)$, minimized by the mode); Bayes error rate; (b) the generative decision rule $\hat y = \arg\max_y p_\theta(x\mid y)p_\theta(y)$ from Bayes' rule, denominator irrelevant; (c) MLE of a generative model factorizes: $\sum_i \log p_\theta(x^{(i)}, y^{(i)}) = \sum_i \log p(x^{(i)} \mid y^{(i)}) + \sum_i \log p(y^{(i)})$ so prior and class-conditional parameters are fit separately. *Visualizations:* two class-conditional densities with the posterior curve overlaid and the Bayes boundary; same data fit discriminatively vs generatively. *Pitfalls:* "generative models are always worse"; believing we can know the Bayes error; forgetting the prior when classes are imbalanced. *Refs:* [AML Lecture 6](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture6-naive-bayes.html); CS229 ch. 4 intro; PML1 §9.1; ESL §2.4; Ng & Jordan 2002 (generative vs discriminative) as optional.

**(11) Naive Bayes.**
*Notation:* bag of words $\phi(x) \in \{0,1\}^V$ (Bernoulli) or counts; class prior $\phi_k$; Bernoulli NB $\psi_{jk} = P(x_j = 1 \mid y = k)$; Multinomial NB $\psi_{wk} = P(\text{word} = w \mid y = k)$ with $\sum_w \psi_{wk} = 1$; conditional independence $p(x \mid y) = \prod_j p(x_j \mid y)$. *Derive:* (a) **MLE of Bernoulli NB**: log-likelihood $\sum_i [\log\phi_{y^{(i)}} + \sum_j (x_j^{(i)}\log\psi_{j y^{(i)}} + (1 - x_j^{(i)})\log(1 - \psi_{j y^{(i)}}))]$; it decomposes per $(j, k)$ into coin-flip problems (reuse (5a)) ⇒ $\hat\psi_{jk} = \frac{\#\{i : y^{(i)} = k, x_j^{(i)} = 1\}}{\#\{i : y^{(i)} = k\}}$; (b) $\hat\phi_k = n_k / n$ via Lagrange multiplier for the simplex constraint (sticky-note); (c) **Laplace/add-one smoothing** $\hat\psi_{jk} = \frac{\#(\cdot) + 1}{n_k + 2}$ (Bernoulli) or $\frac{\mathrm{count} + 1}{\mathrm{total} + V}$ (Multinomial) and its MAP interpretation with a Beta(2,2)/Dirichlet prior; (d) prediction in log space with the log-sum-exp trick (numerical sticky-note); (e) NB posterior is linear in $x$ for Bernoulli features: $\log\frac{p(y=1 \mid x)}{p(y=0 \mid x)} = \theta^\top x$ — foreshadows (12). *Visualizations:* word-probability bars per class; "why zero probabilities kill you" demo with/without smoothing; the spam-notebook confusion matrix (course has `NaiveBayes_Spam_exercise`). *Pitfalls:* Bernoulli vs multinomial event models; underflow without logs; independence assumption is false yet NB often works (why: decision only needs the argmax); applying NB to continuous features without Gaussian NB. *Refs:* [AML Lecture 6](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture6-naive-bayes.html); CS229 §4.2 (incl. Laplace smoothing, event models); PML1 §9.3; PRML §4.2.

**(12) Gaussian Discriminant Analysis / LDA & QDA.**
*Notation:* $x \mid y = k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$, $y \sim \mathrm{Cat}(\phi)$; density $\mathcal{N}(x; \mu, \Sigma) = (2\pi)^{-d/2}|\Sigma|^{-1/2}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}(x - \mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(x - \mu))$; LDA shares $\Sigma$; QDA has $\Sigma_k$. *Derive:* (a) **MLE of $\phi_k, \mu_k$** (reuse (10c); $\mu_k$ via $\nabla_\mu$ of the quadratic form); (b) **shared covariance** $\hat\Sigma = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i (x^{(i)} - \mu_{y^{(i)}})(x^{(i)} - \mu_{y^{(i)}})^\top$ using the trace trick $\nabla_\Sigma[\log|\Sigma| + \mathrm{tr}(\Sigma^{-1}S)]$ (collapsed; matrix-calculus sticky-notes $\nabla \log|\Sigma| = \Sigma^{-\top}$, $\nabla \mathrm{tr}(\Sigma^{-1}S) = -\Sigma^{-\top}S\Sigma^{-\top}$); (c) **the posterior is a sigmoid of a linear function**: $p(y = 1 \mid x) = \sigma(\theta^\top x + \theta_0)$ with $\theta = \Sigma^{-1}(\mu_1 - \mu_0)$, $\theta_0 = -\frac{1}{2}\mu_1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mu_1 + \frac{1}{2}\mu_0^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mu_0 + \log\frac{\phi_1}{\phi_0}$ — steps: write the log posterior odds, expand both quadratics, the $x^\top\Sigma^{-1}x$ terms cancel because $\Sigma$ is shared, collect linear and constant terms; (d) QDA boundary is quadratic (the $x^\top(\Sigma_0^{-1} - \Sigma_1^{-1})x$ term survives); (e) relation to logistic regression: GDA ⇒ logistic form but not conversely; GDA is more data-efficient if the Gaussian assumption holds, logistic regression more robust (CS229's discussion). *Visualizations:* two Gaussian ellipses with the linear LDA boundary, then unequal covariances giving a curved QDA boundary; posterior surface; "assumption violated" dataset where logistic regression wins. *Pitfalls:* $\Sigma$ singular when $d > n$ (shrinkage/regularized LDA); confusing LDA (discriminant) with latent Dirichlet allocation; thinking the sigmoid form implies the same fitted $\theta$ as logistic regression. *Refs:* [AML Lecture 7](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture7-gaussian-discriminant-analysis.html); CS229 §4.1 (GDA, GDA vs logistic); ESL §4.3; PML1 §9.2; MML §6.5 (Gaussian facts).

**(13) Decision trees and random forests.**
*Notation:* tree $T$ partitions $\mathcal{X}$ into regions $R_1,\dots,R_M$; leaf prediction $\hat c_m$; split on feature $j$ at threshold $s$: $R_L = \{x : x_j \le s\}$, $R_R$; node class proportions $\hat p_{mk}$; impurity: Gini $G = \sum_k \hat p_{mk}(1 - \hat p_{mk})$, entropy $H = -\sum_k \hat p_{mk}\log\hat p_{mk}$, misclassification $1 - \max_k \hat p_{mk}$; regression: variance / SSE reduction. *Derive:* (a) CART greedy split: $\min_{j, s} [\sum_{i \in R_L}(y^{(i)} - \bar y_L)^2 + \sum_{i \in R_R}(y^{(i)} - \bar y_R)^2]$, with the leaf mean being optimal for squared loss (one line); information gain $= H(\text{parent}) - \sum_{\text{child}} \frac{n_c}{n}H(c)$; (b) why Gini and entropy are preferred over misclassification for growing (strict concavity rewards purer children); (c) **bagging reduces variance**: average of $B$ predictors with pairwise correlation $\rho$ and variance $\sigma^2$ has variance $\rho\sigma^2 + \frac{1 - \rho}{B}\sigma^2$ (ESL eq. 15.1 derivation, 4 steps) — motivates random feature subsets ($m \approx \sqrt{d}$) to lower $\rho$; (d) out-of-bag error as free CV; (e) feature importance: mean impurity decrease and permutation importance; cost-complexity pruning $\sum_m \mathrm{SSE}_m + \alpha|T|$. *Visualizations:* axis-aligned partition of a 2-D clinical feature plane evolving split by split; impurity functions on $[0,1]$; forest decision surface smoothing as $B$ grows; OOB curve. *Pitfalls:* trees extrapolate as constants; importance biased toward high-cardinality features; "random forests can't overfit"; deep trees + tiny leaves; permutation importance with correlated features. *Refs:* [AML Lecture 15](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture15-decision-trees.html); ESL §9.2, ch. 15; [MLU-Explain decision trees](https://mlu-explain.github.io/decision-tree/), [random forest](https://mlu-explain.github.io/random-forest/); PML1 ch. 18; CS229 "Decision Trees" supplementary notes.

**(14) k-means.**
*Notation:* clusters $k = 1..K$; centroids $c_k$ (slides) / $\mu_k$ (CS229); assignments $z^{(i)} \in \{1..K\}$ or $f(x^{(i)})$; objective $J(c, z) = \sum_i \|x^{(i)} - c_{z^{(i)}}\|^2$ (within-cluster SSE / distortion). *Derive:* (a) **Lloyd's algorithm as coordinate descent**: fixing $c$, the optimal $z^{(i)} = \arg\min_k\|x^{(i)} - c_k\|^2$; fixing $z$, the optimal $c_k$ is the cluster mean (set $\nabla_{c_k} J = 0$); each step does not increase $J$ and $J \ge 0$ with finitely many partitions ⇒ convergence to a local optimum (not global); (b) **k-means++** seeding: pick next center with probability $\propto D(x)^2$; state the $O(\log K)$-competitive guarantee (Arthur & Vassilvitskii 2007; collapsed); (c) elbow / silhouette for choosing $K$; (d) k-means is the $\sigma \to 0$ limit of GMM-EM with spherical equal covariances (done in (15)). *Visualizations:* step-by-step Lloyd animation on 2-D patient features; bad local optimum from poor init vs k-means++; $J$ vs $K$ elbow; Voronoi cells. *Pitfalls:* assumes spherical equal-size clusters; sensitive to scaling; $J$ always decreases with $K$ so the elbow is heuristic; clusters are not "real" subgroups without validation. *Refs:* [AML Lecture 10](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture10-clustering.html); CS229 ch. 10 (k-means); ESL §14.3; PML1 §21.3; MML (none directly; use ch. 11 for GMM link).

**(15) Gaussian mixture models and EM.**
*Notation:* latent $z^{(i)} \sim \mathrm{Cat}(\pi)$, $x^{(i)} \mid z^{(i)} = k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$; marginal $p(x) = \sum_k \pi_k\mathcal{N}(x; \mu_k, \Sigma_k)$ (the L9/L10 slide "$P(x) = \sum_k P(k)\mathcal{N}(x; \mu_k, \sigma_k^2)$"); responsibilities $w_k^{(i)} = Q_i(z^{(i)} = k) = p(z^{(i)} = k \mid x^{(i)}; \theta)$; ELBO $\mathcal{L}(Q, \theta)$. *Derive:* (a) why direct MLE is hard (log of a sum); (b) **ELBO via Jensen**: $\log p(x) = \log\sum_z Q(z)\frac{p(x, z)}{Q(z)} \ge \sum_z Q(z)\log\frac{p(x, z)}{Q(z)} = \mathcal{L}(Q, \theta)$, with equality iff $Q(z) = p(z \mid x; \theta)$; also the identity $\log p(x) = \mathcal{L}(Q, \theta) + D(Q \,\|\, p(z \mid x))$; (c) **E-step** sets $Q_i = p(z^{(i)} \mid x^{(i)}; \theta^{\text{old}})$ (tightens the bound); **M-step** maximizes $\sum_i\sum_k w_k^{(i)}\log\frac{p(x^{(i)}, z^{(i)} = k; \theta)}{w_k^{(i)}}$ over $\theta$ — for GMM this is a weighted version of the GDA MLE from (12): $\pi_k = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i w_k^{(i)}$, $\mu_k = \frac{\sum_i w_k^{(i)}x^{(i)}}{\sum_i w_k^{(i)}}$, $\Sigma_k = \frac{\sum_i w_k^{(i)}(x^{(i)} - \mu_k)(x^{(i)} - \mu_k)^\top}{\sum_i w_k^{(i)}}$; (d) **monotonicity**: $\log p(x; \theta^{t+1}) \ge \mathcal{L}(Q^t, \theta^{t+1}) \ge \mathcal{L}(Q^t, \theta^t) = \log p(x; \theta^t)$; (e) **k-means as a limit**: with $\Sigma_k = \sigma^2 I$, $\sigma^2 \to 0$ responsibilities become hard argmin assignments and the M-step becomes centroid means. *Visualizations:* EM iterations with ellipses tightening on 2-D data; responsibilities as point colors blending; ELBO vs iteration staircase ("bound then maximize" figure from Bishop 9.14); GDA ↔ GMM side-by-side (same formulas, labels known vs inferred). *Pitfalls:* singularities (a component collapsing on a point → add a covariance floor); local optima (init with k-means); label switching; choosing $K$ by log-likelihood alone (always increases → use BIC/held-out). *Refs:* [AML Lecture 10](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture10-clustering.html); CS229 ch. 10–11 (mixtures, EM); PRML ch. 9 (the canonical treatment); MML ch. 11; PML1 §8.7.

**(16) Density estimation.**
*Notation:* histogram with bin width $h$: $\hat p(x) = \frac{\#\{x^{(i)} \in \text{bin}(x)\}}{nh}$; KDE $\hat p_h(x) = \frac{1}{nh}\sum_i K(\frac{x - x^{(i)}}{h})$ with kernel $K \ge 0$, $\int K = 1$ (Gaussian, Epanechnikov); bandwidth $h$; multivariate $\frac{1}{n h^d}$. *Derive:* (a) KDE integrates to 1 (substitution); (b) histogram and KDE as the limit of smoothing — bias $O(h^2)$ and variance $O(1/(nh))$, so the MSE-optimal $h \propto n^{-1/5}$ (sketch; Silverman's rule $h = 1.06\hat\sigma n^{-1/5}$ as a formula to know); (c) likelihood-based bandwidth choice by leave-one-out CV (why in-sample likelihood picks $h \to 0$); (d) parametric (Gaussian MLE, (5b)) vs nonparametric vs mixture (15) as a spectrum. *Visualizations:* bandwidth slider from spiky to oversmoothed; kernels stacked on data points summing into the curve; histogram bin-origin sensitivity. *Pitfalls:* the curse of dimensionality (KDE is hopeless beyond ~5 dims); boundary bias on bounded supports (lab values ≥ 0); using KDE for outlier detection without checking $h$. *Refs:* [AML Lecture 9](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture9-density-estimation.html); ESL §6.6; PML1 §16.3; PRML §2.5.

**(17) PCA.**
*Notation:* centered data matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$; covariance $S = \frac{1}{n}X^\top X$; principal directions $u_1,\dots,u_m$ (orthonormal); scores $z^{(i)} = U^\top x^{(i)}$; reconstruction $\hat x = UU^\top x$; SVD $X = U\Sigma V^\top$ (watch the symbol clash: $\Sigma$ here is the singular-value matrix, not a covariance — sticky-note); explained variance ratio $\lambda_j/\sum_j\lambda_j$. *Derive:* (a) **variance maximization**: $\max_{\|u\| = 1} u^\top S u$ via Lagrangian ⇒ $Su = \lambda u$, the maximizer is the top eigenvector and the maximized variance is $\lambda_1$; induction with orthogonality constraints for subsequent directions (MML ch. 10 does this cleanly); (b) **reconstruction-error minimization** $\min_U \sum_i\|x^{(i)} - UU^\top x^{(i)}\|^2 = \mathrm{tr}(S) - \mathrm{tr}(U^\top S U)$ gives the same eigenvectors — the two views are equivalent; (c) **SVD connection**: right singular vectors of $X$ are eigenvectors of $X^\top X$ and $\lambda_j = \sigma_j^2/n$; scores are $U\Sigma$; low-rank Eckart–Young statement (collapsed); (d) scree plot and the cumulative-variance rule; (e) PCA as a linear autoencoder / preview to MLPs; whitening. *Visualizations:* 2-D cloud with the rotating unit vector and projected variance; 3-D → 2-D projection; scree plot; reconstructions of gene-expression profiles at $m = 1, 2, 5, 20$; the "eigenpatients" heatmap. *Pitfalls:* forgetting to center (and usually standardize); PCA is unsupervised — top PCs need not be predictive; sign/rotation ambiguity; interpreting components causally; computing $X^\top X$ explicitly when $d$ is huge (use SVD). *Refs:* [AML Lecture 8](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/aml-book/contents/lecture8-unsupervised-learning.html); MML ch. 10 (the best free derivation); CS229 ch. 12 (PCA); ESL §14.5; [d2l 22.2 Eigendecompositions](https://d2l.ai/chapter_appendix-mathematics-for-deep-learning/eigendecomposition.html); PRML §12.1.

**(18) Multilayer perceptrons and backpropagation.**
*Notation (d2l style, since d2l is the textbook):* layer $l$ pre-activation $z^{[l]} = W^{[l]}a^{[l-1]} + b^{[l]}$, activation $a^{[l]} = g(z^{[l]})$, $a^{[0]} = x$; output $\hat y = a^{[L]}$; loss $\ell(\hat y, y)$; error signal $\delta^{[l]} = \frac{\partial\ell}{\partial z^{[l]}}$; activations ReLU $\max(0, z)$, sigmoid, tanh, GELU; width, depth. Map explicitly to course $\theta$ (= all $W, b$) and $f_\theta$. *Derive:* (a) a linear network of any depth collapses to a linear model (motivates nonlinearity); (b) XOR is not linearly separable but a 2-unit hidden layer solves it (concrete weights); (c) **universal approximation intuition**: sums of shifted sigmoids/ReLUs build bump functions in 1-D; theorem statement only (Cybenko/Hornik; collapsed); (d) **backprop for a 2-layer network via the chain rule**, fully: $\delta^{[L]} = \nabla_{\hat y}\ell \odot g'(z^{[L]})$ (or simply $\hat y - y$ for softmax/CE and sigmoid/BCE — show this simplification, it reuses (4d)); $\delta^{[l]} = (W^{[l+1]\top}\delta^{[l+1]}) \odot g'(z^{[l]})$; $\nabla_{W^{[l]}}\ell = \delta^{[l]}a^{[l-1]\top}$, $\nabla_{b^{[l]}}\ell = \delta^{[l]}$; count the cost: one forward + one backward pass is $O(\#\text{weights})$ versus finite differences $O(\#\text{weights}^2)$; (e) computational graphs and reverse-mode autodiff as the general statement; (f) **initialization**: variance of $z^{[l]}$ grows/shrinks geometrically unless $\mathrm{Var}(W) = 1/n_{\text{in}}$ (Xavier) or $2/n_{\text{in}}$ (He) — derive the variance-propagation recursion in 4 steps; vanishing gradients with sigmoid ($\sigma' \le 1/4$); (g) softmax + CE gradient and the log-sum-exp trick. *Visualizations:* a 1-D function being built from ReLU bumps with sliders; the computational graph with gradients flowing backward (CS231n "gates"); loss landscape 2-D slice; activation and gradient histograms per layer for bad vs good init. *Pitfalls:* "deeper is always better"; forgetting to shuffle; dead ReLUs; sigmoid output + squared loss (non-convex, slow); mixing up $\partial\ell/\partial z$ and $\partial\ell/\partial a$; initializing all weights to zero (symmetry never breaks). *Refs:* [d2l 5.1 MLP](https://d2l.ai/chapter_multilayer-perceptrons/mlp.html), [5.3 Backprop](https://d2l.ai/chapter_multilayer-perceptrons/backprop.html), [5.4 Numerical stability and initialization](https://d2l.ai/chapter_multilayer-perceptrons/numerical-stability-and-init.html); [CS231n backprop notes](https://cs231n.github.io/optimization-2/) and [neural nets](https://cs231n.github.io/neural-networks-1/); CS229 ch. 7 (deep learning); PML1 ch. 13.

**(19) CNNs.**
*Notation:* input tensor $c_{\text{in}} \times H \times W$; kernel/filter $K \in \mathbb{R}^{c_{\text{out}} \times c_{\text{in}} \times k \times k}$; (cross-)correlation $(X \star K)[i, j] = \sum_{a, b} X[i + a, j + b]K[a, b]$; stride $s$, padding $p$; output size $\lfloor\frac{H + 2p - k}{s}\rfloor + 1$; pooling (max/average); receptive field. *Derive:* (a) **convolution as a constrained fully connected layer**: translation equivariance + locality ⇒ weight sharing; parameter count $c_{\text{out}}c_{\text{in}}k^2$ vs $(HW)^2$ for dense (d2l 7.1's derivation from an MLP on images); (b) output-size formula; (c) receptive-field recursion $r_{l} = r_{l-1} + (k_l - 1)\prod_{j < l}s_j$; (d) backprop through a conv layer is a conv with the flipped kernel (sketch) and through max-pool routes gradient to the argmax; (e) why pooling gives (approximate) translation invariance; (f) classic architectures: LeNet-5 → AlexNet → VGG → ResNet (skip connections: $a^{[l+2]} = g(z^{[l+2]} + a^{[l]})$ and why it eases optimization — gradient has an identity path). *Visualizations:* a kernel sliding over a synthetic chest-X-ray-like image with the output map painting in real time; filters as edge detectors; receptive-field growth diagram; feature maps per layer. *Pitfalls:* "convolution" in DL is cross-correlation; counting parameters incorrectly (forgetting channels/bias); believing CNNs are rotation invariant; using fully connected layers on raw pixels. *Refs:* [d2l 7.1](https://d2l.ai/chapter_convolutional-neural-networks/why-conv.html), [7.2](https://d2l.ai/chapter_convolutional-neural-networks/conv-layer.html), [7.3](https://d2l.ai/chapter_convolutional-neural-networks/padding-and-strides.html), [7.5 Pooling](https://d2l.ai/chapter_convolutional-neural-networks/pooling.html), [7.6 LeNet](https://d2l.ai/chapter_convolutional-neural-networks/lenet.html), d2l ch. 8 (modern CNNs); [CS231n CNN notes](https://cs231n.github.io/convolutional-networks/); PML1 §14.

**(20a) Transformers / LLM basics (brief).**
*Notation:* tokens → embeddings $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$; queries/keys/values $Q = XW_Q$, $K = XW_K$, $V = XW_V$; scaled dot-product attention $\mathrm{softmax}(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}})V$; multi-head; positional encoding $PE_{(t, 2i)} = \sin(t/10000^{2i/d})$, $PE_{(t, 2i+1)} = \cos(\cdot)$; causal mask; next-token cross-entropy. *Derive:* (a) attention as a differentiable soft dictionary lookup (kernel-regression / Nadaraya–Watson view in d2l 11.2); (b) **why $\sqrt{d_k}$**: if $q, k$ have i.i.d. unit-variance entries, $\mathrm{Var}(q^\top k) = d_k$, so without scaling softmax saturates and gradients vanish (3 steps); (c) softmax Jacobian $\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j} = p_i(\delta_{ij} - p_j)$ (reuses $\sigma'$); (d) why positional encodings are needed (attention is permutation-equivariant — one line) and the sinusoidal encoding's relative-shift property ($PE_{t + \Delta}$ is a linear function of $PE_t$); (e) LLM training objective as MLE of a sequence model (reuses (5)). *Visualizations:* attention heat-map over a clinical note; the $\sqrt{d_k}$ saturation plot; sinusoidal PE heatmap. *Pitfalls:* attention weights ≠ explanations; confusing encoder/decoder masks; thinking context length is free (quadratic cost). *Refs:* [d2l 11.1 Queries/keys/values](https://d2l.ai/chapter_attention-mechanisms-and-transformers/queries-keys-values.html), [11.6 Self-attention and positional encoding](https://d2l.ai/chapter_attention-mechanisms-and-transformers/self-attention-and-positional-encoding.html), [11.7 Transformer](https://d2l.ai/chapter_attention-mechanisms-and-transformers/transformer.html); Vaswani et al. 2017; PML1 §15.4–15.5.

**(20b) Reinforcement learning basics (brief).**
*Notation:* MDP $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, P, r, \gamma)$; policy $\pi(a \mid s)$; return $G_t = \sum_k\gamma^k r_{t+k}$; $V^\pi(s)$, $Q^\pi(s, a)$; optimal $V^*, Q^*$; bandit as a one-state MDP. *Derive:* (a) **Bellman expectation equation** $V^\pi(s) = \sum_a\pi(a \mid s)[r(s, a) + \gamma\sum_{s'}P(s' \mid s, a)V^\pi(s')]$ by splitting $G_t = r_t + \gamma G_{t+1}$ and using the Markov property; (b) **Bellman optimality** $V^*(s) = \max_a[r + \gamma\mathbb{E}V^*(s')]$ and value iteration as a $\gamma$-contraction (collapsed Banach argument); (c) Q-learning update $Q(s, a) \leftarrow Q(s, a) + \alpha[r + \gamma\max_{a'}Q(s', a') - Q(s, a)]$ as SGD on the Bellman residual; (d) **policy gradient theorem / REINFORCE**: $\nabla_\theta\mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta}[G] = \mathbb{E}[\sum_t\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)G_t]$ via the log-derivative trick $\nabla p = p\nabla\log p$ (5 steps) and a baseline for variance reduction; (e) bandits: UCB and Thompson sampling with Beta–Bernoulli conjugacy (posterior update is add-one counting — reuses (11c)'s Beta prior) — this is the natural bridge to response-adaptive trials. *Visualizations:* grid-world value iteration heat-map; a 3-arm Beta posterior animation as patients are enrolled; regret curves. *Pitfalls:* confusing reward and return; "greedy is optimal"; exploration in trials has ethical constraints (equal-allocation floors); off-policy vs on-policy. *Refs:* [d2l 17.1 MDP](https://d2l.ai/chapter_reinforcement-learning/mdp.html), [17.2 Value iteration](https://d2l.ai/chapter_reinforcement-learning/value-iter.html), [17.3 Q-learning](https://d2l.ai/chapter_reinforcement-learning/qlearning.html); CS229 ch. 15–17 (RL, policy gradient); Sutton & Barto (free) ch. 2–4, 13; for adaptive trials: [I-SPY2 Thompson-sampling design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16047v1), [HeartSteps MRT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6401341).

### Sticky-note prerequisite glossary (~72 terms)

Each: one-line definition and the ML unit where the platform first needs it (so it can be surfaced inline).

**Probability (20)**
1. Random variable — a function from outcomes to numbers; the slides' $X : \Omega \to E$. First: L3.
2. PMF / PDF — $P(X = x)$ for discrete; density with $P(a \le X \le b) = \int_a^b p$ for continuous. First: L3/L6.
3. Joint, marginal, conditional — $p(x, y)$; $p(x) = \sum_y p(x, y)$; $p(y \mid x) = p(x, y)/p(x)$. First: L4 (true risk), L5.
4. Independence / i.i.d. — $p(x, y) = p(x)p(y)$; samples drawn independently from the same $P$. First: L4, used in every likelihood product.
5. Conditional independence — $p(x_1, x_2 \mid y) = p(x_1 \mid y)p(x_2 \mid y)$. First: L9 Naive Bayes.
6. Expectation and linearity — $\mathbb{E}[aX + bY] = a\mathbb{E}X + b\mathbb{E}Y$ always. First: L4 risk; L8 bias–variance.
7. Variance, covariance, covariance matrix — $\mathrm{Var}X = \mathbb{E}[(X - \mu)^2]$; $\Sigma = \mathbb{E}[(x - \mu)(x - \mu)^\top]$ is symmetric PSD. First: L8, L10 GDA.
8. Law of total expectation / tower property — $\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[Y \mid X]] = \mathbb{E}Y$. First: Bayes-optimal predictor (L4), bias–variance.
9. Bayes' rule — $p(y \mid x) = p(x \mid y)p(y)/p(x)$. First: L8/L9 generative models.
10. Prior, likelihood, posterior, evidence — the four pieces of Bayes' rule named. First: L8 MAP, L9.
11. Bernoulli and Binomial — $P(x = 1) = \theta$; counts of successes. First: L5 coin flip.
12. Categorical and Multinomial — $K$-sided die with $\sum_k\phi_k = 1$; word counts. First: L9.
13. Gaussian (univariate) — $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ density and its log. First: L6 noise model.
14. Multivariate Gaussian — $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$ density, $|\Sigma|$, $\Sigma^{-1}$, ellipsoidal contours. First: L10 GDA.
15. Beta and Dirichlet — conjugate priors for Bernoulli/Categorical; mean $\alpha/(\alpha + \beta)$. First: Laplace smoothing (L10), Thompson sampling (RL).
16. Laplace distribution — $\propto e^{-|x|/b}$; the prior behind lasso. First: L8 MAP.
17. Mixture distribution — $p(x) = \sum_k\pi_k p_k(x)$. First: L10 preview, GMM.
18. Law of large numbers — sample means converge to expectations; why empirical risk approximates true risk. First: L4.
19. Central limit theorem — standardized sums are approximately Gaussian; why Gaussian noise is a default. First: L6.
20. Jensen's inequality — for convex $f$, $f(\mathbb{E}X) \le \mathbb{E}f(X)$ (reverse for concave, e.g. $\log$). First: KL ≥ 0 (L7), EM.

**Statistics (12)**
21. Estimator, bias, variance, MSE — $\mathrm{MSE} = \mathrm{bias}^2 + \mathrm{variance}$. First: L8.
22. Unbiased estimator — $\mathbb{E}\hat\theta = \theta$; sample variance's $n - 1$. First: L4/L5.
23. Consistency — $\hat\theta_n \to \theta$ in probability. First: L5 MLE.
24. Sample mean / sample covariance — the MLE formulas for Gaussians. First: L5, L10.
25. Likelihood vs probability — same formula, different argument held fixed. First: L5.
26. Log-likelihood — turns products into sums; monotone so argmax is unchanged. First: L5.
27. Sufficient statistic — e.g. $\#H$ for the coin; counts for NB. First: L5, L9.
28. MAP estimate — $\arg\max p(\theta \mid \mathcal{D})$. First: L8.
29. Conjugate prior — posterior in the same family (Beta–Bernoulli). First: L10 smoothing, RL bandits.
30. Hypothesis test, p-value, confidence interval — one-line refreshers because clinical examples quote them. First: Part C trials; L8 CV.
31. Prevalence / base rate — $P(y = 1)$; drives PPV and the all-negative baseline. First: L7.
32. Sensitivity / specificity / PPV / NPV — clinical names for recall, TNR, precision, and its negative twin. First: L7.

**Linear algebra (18)**
33. Vector, dot product, norm — $\|x\|_2^2 = x^\top x$; $\|x\|_1 = \sum|x_j|$. First: L2, L8.
34. Matrix–vector product as linear map; column space. First: L2 design matrix.
35. Transpose rules — $(AB)^\top = B^\top A^\top$. First: normal equations.
36. Inverse; when it exists — full rank, nonzero determinant. First: normal equations.
37. Rank, linear independence, collinearity — why $X^\top X$ can be singular. First: L2/L8.
38. Symmetric matrices and the spectral theorem — $S = V\Lambda V^\top$ with orthonormal $V$. First: condition number (L5), ridge, PCA.
39. Positive (semi)definite — $x^\top Ax \ge 0$ for all $x$; all eigenvalues $\ge 0$; Hessian test for convexity. First: L4/L5.
40. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors — $Av = \lambda v$. First: L5 condition number, PCA.
41. Condition number — $\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$; elongated contours. First: L5.
42. Orthogonal projection and projection matrix — $H = X(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top$, $H^2 = H$. First: L2.
43. Trace and its cyclic property — $\mathrm{tr}(ABC) = \mathrm{tr}(CAB)$. First: GDA covariance MLE, PCA.
44. Determinant — volume scaling; $\log|\Sigma|$ in the Gaussian. First: L10.
45. Quadratic form — $x^\top Ax$; its gradient $2Ax$ for symmetric $A$. First: L2.
46. SVD — $X = U\Sigma V^\top$; singular values. First: PCA.
47. Lagrange multipliers — $\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$ on a constraint. First: $\hat\phi_k$ in NB, PCA.
48. Outer product — $xx^\top$; building covariance. First: L10, backprop ($\delta a^\top$).
49. Hadamard (elementwise) product $\odot$. First: backprop.
50. Tensor shapes / broadcasting — $(n \times d)(d \times 1)$; batch dimension. First: code companions, CNNs.

**Calculus and optimization (14)**
51. Derivative as local slope; second derivative as curvature. First: L4.
52. Partial derivative and gradient — column vector of partials; direction of steepest ascent. First: L4.
53. Chain rule (scalar and multivariate) — $\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))g'(x)$; Jacobians compose. First: L5 ($\sigma'$), backprop.
54. Hessian — matrix of second partials; PSD ⇒ convex. First: L4.
55. Matrix calculus identities — $\nabla_\theta b^\top\theta = b$; $\nabla_\theta\theta^\top A\theta = 2A\theta$; $\nabla_\Sigma\log|\Sigma| = \Sigma^{-1}$. First: L2, L10.
56. Stationary points; first- and second-order conditions. First: L4.
57. Convex set and convex function — definitions and the "chord above graph" picture. First: L4/L5.
58. Lipschitz gradient ($L$-smooth) and strong convexity — bound the curvature from above/below. First: GD convergence.
59. Subgradient — generalizes the derivative at kinks ($|x|$ at 0). First: lasso.
60. Taylor expansion to second order — basis of the descent lemma and Newton's method. First: GD.
61. Log-sum-exp and numerical stability — $\log\sum e^{z_i} = m + \log\sum e^{z_i - m}$. First: NB prediction, softmax.
62. Learning-rate schedules — constant, decay, warm-up (names only). First: L5.
63. Coordinate descent — optimize one block holding others fixed. First: k-means, EM.
64. Soft-thresholding operator — $(|x| - \lambda)_+\mathrm{sign}(x)$. First: lasso.

**Information theory (8)**
65. Entropy $H(p) = -\sum p\log p$ — expected surprise; max for uniform. First: L7, decision trees.
66. Cross-entropy $H(p, q)$ — expected code length using $q$ for data from $p$. First: L5/L7.
67. KL divergence — $H(p, q) - H(p)$; non-negative, asymmetric. First: L6/L7.

## PART C — Clinical-trial-flavored running examples

All datasets are synthetic, generated from stated models with fixed seeds, so every number in a lesson is reproducible and the "truth" is known (bias and variance can be shown exactly). Parameter values are chosen to be physiologically plausible and to echo the course's existing diabetes example. Patient IDs are shared across examples so the same cohort reappears ("Patient 0042 from the BP trial shows up in the clustering unit").

**C1. "VASCO" dose-finding trial: blood-pressure reduction vs dose (regression backbone).**
*Variables:* dose $D \in \{0, 2.5, 5, 10, 20, 40\}$ mg; baseline SBP $B$; age; sex; outcome $\Delta\mathrm{SBP}$ at 8 weeks (negative = improvement).
*Generative story:* Emax model, the standard pharmacometric dose–response: $\Delta\mathrm{SBP} = E_0 + \frac{E_{\max}D}{ED_{50} + D} + 0.15(B - 150) + \epsilon$ with $E_0 = -3$ (placebo effect), $E_{\max} = -22$ mmHg, $ED_{50} = 6$ mg, $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 8^2)$; $B \sim \mathcal{N}(155, 12^2)$; $n = 300$ (50 per arm). Realistic $E_{\max}$ values for antihypertensives run −15 to −40 mmHg ([Emax dose–response references](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/clinDR/html/emaxsim.html)).
*Lessons:* L2 linear regression on $\log(1 + D)$ (shows a good linear fit to a nonlinear truth, and the residual plot that reveals it); polynomial features via $\phi$; L4–L5 gradient descent on this loss (feature scaling: dose in mg vs baseline in mmHg gives a terrible condition number — the perfect $\kappa$ demo); L6 Gaussian-noise MLE; L8 ridge/lasso with 20 added noise covariates (lab values) to show lasso recovering {dose, baseline}; bias–variance with polynomial degree; later a 1-hidden-layer MLP recovers the Emax curve.

**C2. "ADVERSE" post-marketing cohort: predicting a serious adverse event (classification backbone).**
*Variables:* age, eGFR (kidney function), potassium, concomitant NSAID (binary), dose from C1, diabetes flag; outcome $y = 1$ if hyperkalemia event within 90 days.
*Generative story:* $P(y = 1 \mid x) = \sigma(-4.5 + 0.03(\text{age} - 60) - 0.04(\text{eGFR} - 70) + 1.2\cdot\text{NSAID} + 0.6\cdot\mathbb{1}[\text{K} > 5.0] + 0.05 D)$; prevalence ≈ 6%; $n = 5{,}000$. Add a *known* nonlinearity (risk rises sharply below eGFR 30) so trees beat logistic regression later.
*Lessons:* L5 logistic regression (log-odds are literally the model), gradient and convexity; L6 Bernoulli MLE; L7 evaluation with 6% prevalence (accuracy of all-negative = 94%), threshold choice with cost of a missed event ≫ cost of a false alarm, ROC vs PR; calibration; L8 regularization against perfect separation when we add a leaky feature; L10 GDA on (age, eGFR) vs logistic regression; decision trees/random forests on the eGFR-30 kink; feature importance.

**C3. "TROPO" diagnostic test: high-sensitivity troponin for myocardial infarction (ROC/threshold backbone).**
*Variables:* troponin level (ng/L, log-normal), MI status, sex, time since symptom onset.
*Generative story:* $\log T \mid \text{MI} = 0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\log 8, 0.6^2)$; $\log T \mid \text{MI} = 1 \sim \mathcal{N}(\log 60, 0.9^2)$; MI prevalence 15% in the ED cohort; $n = 2{,}000$. Two overlapping log-normals make the two-histogram-plus-slider explorable exact.
*Lessons:* L7 confusion matrix, sensitivity/specificity, the rule-out vs rule-in thresholds (sensitivity-first at 5 ng/L vs specificity-first at 52 ng/L), AUC's probabilistic meaning, PPV changes with prevalence (ED vs primary care at 2%); L10 GDA in 1-D (two Gaussians on $\log T$ ⇒ the posterior is a sigmoid: derive the exact $\theta$ from the parameters above and compare to a fitted logistic regression); L16 KDE of troponin by class; Bayes-optimal threshold with costs.

**C4. "SEPSIS-4" ICU phenotypes (clustering / GMM / density backbone).**
*Variables:* 8 continuous features at ICU admission: age, heart rate, temperature, lactate, creatinine, bilirubin, SpO₂/FiO₂, vasopressor dose; 28-day mortality (held out, used only for validation).
*Generative story:* 4-component Gaussian mixture mirroring the published α/β/γ/δ sepsis phenotypes with weights (0.33, 0.27, 0.27, 0.13) and mortalities (5%, 13%, 24%, 40%) ([Seymour et al. 2019, JAMA](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/machine-learning-reveals-four-types-sepsis)): α = young, low lactate, no pressors; β = older, high creatinine; γ = high temperature/HR, low SpO₂/FiO₂; δ = high lactate, high bilirubin, high pressors. Use non-spherical covariances so k-means and GMM differ visibly; $n = 4{,}000$.
*Lessons:* k-means (Lloyd steps, k-means++, elbow at $K = 4$, failure on elongated clusters); GMM-EM (responsibilities, ellipses, log-likelihood monotonicity, BIC picking $K = 4$); GDA ↔ GMM comparison using the held-out phenotype labels; KDE of lactate; PCA to 2-D for visualization of the clusters; evaluation: mortality by cluster as external validation.

**C5. "LEUK-EXPR" gene-expression panel (PCA / high-dimensional backbone).**
*Variables:* $d = 500$ gene expression levels for $n = 120$ leukemia patients, subtype ALL vs AML; modeled on the Golub 1999 dataset (72 samples × 7,129 genes) ([Golub data description](https://www.openintro.org/data/index.php?data=golub)).
*Generative story:* low-rank + noise: $x^{(i)} = \mu_{y^{(i)}} + Uz^{(i)} + \epsilon$ with $z \in \mathbb{R}^5$ (5 latent biological programs, $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathrm{diag}(9, 6, 3, 1.5, 1))$), $U$ random orthonormal $500 \times 5$, class-mean shift along $u_1$ of magnitude 2, noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.5^2 I)$; 40 genes are pure noise.
*Lessons:* PCA (scree plot shows exactly 5 then a floor; PC1 separates subtypes; reconstruction error vs $m$); SVD when $d > n$; $d > n$ makes $X^\top X$ singular ⇒ ridge (L8) and lasso feature selection; logistic regression on top-$m$ PCs with CV choosing $m$ (model selection with proper nested CV; the leakage demo — select genes on all data and watch CV accuracy lie); Naive Bayes with Gaussian features; random forest importance on genes.

**C6. "NOTES" adverse-event narratives (text / Naive Bayes / transformer backbone).**
*Variables:* short free-text pharmacovigilance reports (synthetic templates: "Patient developed [symptom] [n] days after starting [drug]"); label serious vs non-serious; later ICD chapter (multi-class).
*Generative story:* class-conditional word distributions over a 2,000-word vocabulary: serious reports up-weight {hospitalized, syncope, arrhythmia, ICU, discontinued}; non-serious up-weight {mild, headache, resolved, nausea}; document length Poisson(25); priors 30/70; $n = 6{,}000$. Generated from the NB model itself so NB's MLE provably recovers the parameters (a nice "estimator consistency" demo), then a second version generated with word *dependencies* (bigrams) to show NB's assumption failing.
*Lessons:* bag-of-words $\phi$, Bernoulli vs multinomial NB, add-one smoothing (a held-out word with zero count), log-space prediction; evaluation on imbalanced text; logistic regression on TF-IDF vs NB (discriminative vs generative data-efficiency curve); transformer unit: attention over the same reports, positional encoding, and fine-tuning a small model for the same label.

**C7. "ADAPT-3" response-adaptive three-arm trial (bandits / RL backbone — Prof. Gan's area).**
*Variables:* arms $a \in \{\text{control}, \text{drug A}, \text{drug B}\}$; binary response (e.g. pathologic complete response as in I-SPY 2); patient enrolled sequentially $t = 1..600$; optional biomarker subgroup.
*Generative story:* true response rates $(0.25, 0.32, 0.45)$; Thompson sampling with Beta(1,1) priors, a 10% minimum-allocation floor per arm (ethics constraint), and a burn-in of 30 equal-allocation patients; compare to fixed 1:1:1 randomization on expected responders and on power for the A-vs-control contrast ([I-SPY 2 Thompson-sampling design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16047v1)).
*Lessons:* bandit as a one-state MDP; Beta–Bernoulli posterior update (reuses Laplace smoothing and the coin MLE); regret curves; exploration–exploitation; the price of adaptivity (lower power, biased naive estimates — foreshadows causal inference); MDP/Bellman for a two-stage SMART version (re-randomize non-responders at stage 2, like I-SPY2.2); policy gradient on a simple treatment-policy parametrization.

**C8. "STEPS" micro-randomized mobile-health trial (SGD/online learning + RL backbone; also Gan/Murphy lineage).**
*Variables:* per participant, 5 decision points per day for 42 days; action = send activity suggestion (randomized with $p = 0.6$ when available); context = time of day, location, prior 30-min steps; proximal outcome = steps in the next 30 min — modeled on HeartSteps ([HeartSteps MRT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6401341)).
*Generative story:* $\log(1 + \text{steps}) = 5.5 + 0.14\cdot A_t\cdot e^{-t/30\text{days}} + 0.3\cdot\mathbb{1}[\text{afternoon}] + u_i + \epsilon$ with participant effect $u_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.3^2)$, $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.5^2)$; 40 participants (≈ 8,400 decision points); the treatment effect decays (habituation).
*Lessons:* SGD/mini-batch on a streaming dataset (a natural reason to not use batch GD); grouped cross-validation by participant (leakage demo); linear regression with interaction terms to estimate the treatment effect; contextual bandit / RL unit: learn a policy of *when* to send; "treat the effect size as a learning rate" intuition for the decaying effect; connects directly to the professor's micro-randomized-trial research for motivation.
