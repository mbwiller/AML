<Definition id="def-6-1-1" title="Discriminative model" source="L8 p.30">

A model of the conditional distribution $p_\theta(y \mid \mathbf{x}) : \mathcal{X} \to (\mathcal{Y} \to [0,1])$. It learns the decision boundary and says nothing about $p(\mathbf{x})$.

</Definition>

<Derivation id="der-6-1-1" title="The Bayes classifier" goalTex="\hat y = \argmax_y p(\mathbf{x} \mid y)\, p(y)" resultTex="\hat y = \argmax_y p(\mathbf{x} \mid y)\, p(y)" source="L8 p.39 (two lines)">

<Chunk title="Start from the posterior">

<Step justification="prediction is the most probable label">
$$\hat y = \argmax_y p(y \mid \mathbf{x})$$
</Step>

<Step justification="Bayes' rule" sticky="bayes-rule">
$$= \argmax_y \frac{p(\mathbf{x} \mid y)\, p(y)}{p(\mathbf{x})}$$
</Step>

<Step justification="the denominator does not depend on $y$, so it does not change the argmax" fadeable>
$$= \argmax_y p(\mathbf{x} \mid y)\, p(y)$$
</Step>

</Chunk>

</Derivation>

<Summary>

- Discriminative: $p_\theta(y\mid x)$, learns the boundary. Generative: $p_\theta(x \mid y)\,p_\theta(y)$, learns the data.
- Bayes classifier: $\hat y = \argmax_y p(\mathbf{x}\mid y)\,p(y)$; the prior matters.
- Generative MLE splits: $\sum_i \log p(x^{(i)} \mid y^{(i)}) + \sum_i \log p(y^{(i)})$.

</Summary>

<Intuition>

In one dimension the two class-conditional densities are two bumps; the Bayes classifier picks whichever bump is taller at $x$, after each is scaled by its prior. The boundary is where the scaled bumps cross: $\htmlClass{sym-pos}{p(x \mid 1)\,p(1)} = \htmlClass{sym-neg}{p(x \mid 0)\,p(0)}$.

</Intuition>

<Theorem id="thm-6-1-1" title="The Bayes classifier minimizes the expected 0–1 loss" source="L8 p.40">

For any classifier $f$, $\Prob(f(X) \neq Y) \geq \Prob(f^\star(X) \neq Y)$ where $f^\star(x) = \argmax_y p(y \mid x)$.

</Theorem>

<Proposition id="prop-6-1-1" title="The denominator does not change the argmax">

For fixed $x$, $p(x) > 0$ scales every $p(x \mid y)\,p(y)$ by the same constant.

</Proposition>

<Lemma id="lem-6-1-1" title="Logs preserve the argmax">

$\log$ is strictly increasing, so $\argmax_y g(y) = \argmax_y \log g(y)$ when $g > 0$.

</Lemma>

<Proof collapsed title="The Bayes classifier is optimal" keyIdea="condition on $X = x$ and minimize pointwise">

Write $\Prob(f(X) \neq Y) = \E_X\bigl[\Prob(f(X) \neq Y \mid X)\bigr] = \E_X\bigl[1 - p(f(X) \mid X)\bigr]$.

For each $x$ the integrand $1 - p(f(x) \mid x)$ is smallest when $f(x)$ is the mode of $p(\cdot \mid x)$, which is $f^\star(x)$. Integrating a pointwise minimum gives the global minimum.

</Proof>

$$
p(y \mid x) = \frac{p(x \mid y)\,p(y)}{p(x)}

$$

By <EqRef id="eq-6-1-1" /> the posterior is proportional to the joint.

<Callout type="note" title="A note">

The prior $p(y)$ is a [[categorical-distribution|categorical distribution]] over $K$ classes.

<Callout type="warning">

With $p(y = 1) = 0.9$, a class-conditional likelihood ratio of $0.6 / 0.4$ in favor of class 0 is not enough to predict 0.

</Callout>
