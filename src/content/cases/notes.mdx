When a patient has a reaction to a drug, a clinician or the patient files a short report: "Patient developed syncope 3 days after starting the new medication; hospitalized overnight." Safety teams receive thousands of these and must triage them quickly. The first question is whether a report describes a serious event.

NOTES is a synthetic collection of 6,000 such reports over a 2,000-word vocabulary. Serious reports use words like *hospitalized*, *syncope*, *arrhythmia*, *ICU*, and *discontinued* more often; non-serious reports lean on *mild*, *headache*, *resolved*, and *nausea*. Report length varies around 25 words.

The first version of the data is generated from a Naive Bayes model, so Naive Bayes is exactly right for it: its maximum-likelihood estimates provably recover the true word probabilities as the number of reports grows. A second version adds dependencies between neighboring words, so the Naive Bayes assumption fails and you can watch what that costs.
