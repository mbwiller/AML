**What it is.** Gradient descent with the full gradient of the empirical risk replaced by the gradient on a random sample of the data. With one example $I$ drawn uniformly from $\{1, \dots, n\}$,

$$\theta^{(t+1)} = \theta^{(t)} - \eta_t\, \nabla_\theta \ell\big(f_\theta(x^{(I)}), y^{(I)}\big)\Big|_{\theta = \theta^{(t)}}.$$

Because $I$ is uniform, the expected step equals the full-gradient step: the estimate is noisy but unbiased.

**How ML uses it.** Each step costs one example (or one minibatch) instead of all $n$, so large datasets and data streams become tractable. The noise means a constant step size leaves the iterate jittering near the minimum; decaying the step size removes the jitter. PyTorch's `torch.optim.SGD` and its relatives (Adam, RMSprop) are built on this update.
