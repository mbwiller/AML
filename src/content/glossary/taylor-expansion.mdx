**What it is.** A smooth function looks like a polynomial near a point. In one variable, $f(x + h) \approx f(x) + f'(x)\,h + \tfrac12 f''(x)\,h^2$. In $d$ variables, with gradient $\nabla f$ and Hessian $H$,

$$f(\theta + h) \approx f(\theta) + \nabla f(\theta)^\top h + \tfrac12\, h^\top H(\theta)\, h.$$

Keeping only the first two terms is the linear (first-order) approximation; adding the third gives the quadratic (second-order) one. The error shrinks faster than $\lVert h \rVert^2$ as $h \to 0$ when $f$ is twice continuously differentiable.

**How ML uses it.** Near a minimum the gradient term vanishes, so every smooth loss looks like a quadratic bowl with curvature matrix $H$. That is why the step-size analysis done on quadratics carries over, with the eigenvalues of $H$ playing the role of the curvature.
