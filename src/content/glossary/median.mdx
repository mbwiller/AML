**What it is.** A median of the numbers $y^{(1)}, \dots, y^{(n)}$ is any value $m$ with at least half of the numbers $\le m$ and at least half $\ge m$. Sort them: for odd $n$ it is the middle value; for even $n$ every value between the two middle ones qualifies, and by convention we report their average. Unlike the mean, it does not move when the largest value is made larger still: it depends on the data only through their order near the middle.

**How ML uses it.** The median is the constant that minimizes the mean absolute error, just as the mean minimizes the mean squared error. That is why absolute-error fits resist outliers, and why the best predictor under absolute error is the conditional median of $Y$ given $X$ rather than the conditional mean.
