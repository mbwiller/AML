**What it is.** A prior is conjugate to a likelihood when the posterior lands in the same family as the prior. Updating on data then only changes the family's parameters, usually by adding counts. Standard pairs: a Beta prior with a Bernoulli or binomial likelihood; a Dirichlet prior (the $K$-outcome Beta) with a categorical or multinomial likelihood; a Gaussian prior on the mean with a Gaussian likelihood of known variance.

**How ML uses it.** Conjugacy gives closed-form MAP and posterior estimates, so smoothing becomes "add pseudo-counts". With $s_{jk}$ the number of class-$k$ documents containing word $j$, a Beta prior gives $\hat\psi_{jk} = (s_{jk} + \alpha - 1)/(n_k + \alpha + \beta - 2)$; for multinomial Naive Bayes a Dirichlet($\alpha$) prior adds $\alpha - 1$ to every word count, so $\alpha = 2$ is add-one. Thompson sampling keeps a Beta posterior per bandit arm for the same reason.
