**What it is.** The expected surprise of a draw from a discrete distribution $p$:

$$H(p) = -\sum_x p(x)\log p(x) = \mathbb{E}_{X \sim p}\big[-\log p(X)\big],$$

with $0 \log 0 = 0$. With the natural log it is measured in nats ($\log_2$ gives bits). It is $\ge 0$, it is 0 exactly for a point mass (an outcome that is certain), and among distributions on $m$ outcomes it is largest, $\log m$, for the uniform one.

**How ML uses it.** It is the part of the KL divergence that does not involve the model: $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\,\|\,q_\theta) = H(p, q_\theta) - H(p)$. When $p$ is the data distribution, $H(p)$ is a constant in $\theta$, which is why minimizing cross-entropy and minimizing KL give the same fit. A one-hot label has entropy 0. Decision trees later use entropy to score splits.
