**What it is.** The Beta distribution is a density on $\psi \in [0, 1]$:

$$p(\psi; \alpha, \beta) \propto \psi^{\alpha - 1}(1 - \psi)^{\beta - 1}, \qquad \alpha, \beta > 0.$$

$\alpha = \beta = 1$ is uniform. Read $\alpha - 1$ and $\beta - 1$ as imaginary ones and zeros seen before the data. After $s$ ones in $n$ Bernoulli draws the posterior is $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha + s,\ \beta + n - s)$, and for $\alpha, \beta > 1$ its mode, the MAP estimate, is

$$\hat\psi_{\mathrm{MAP}} = \frac{s + \alpha - 1}{n + \alpha + \beta - 2}.$$

**How ML uses it.** With $\alpha = \beta = 2$ this is $(s + 1)/(n + 2)$: Laplace (add-one) smoothing for Bernoulli Naive Bayes. A word never seen in class $k$ gets a small positive $\hat\psi_{jk}$ instead of 0, which avoids $\log 0$, and the prior's pull fades as $n = n_k$ grows.
