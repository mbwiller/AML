**What it is.** The four factors of Bayes' rule have names. In $p(y \mid x) = p(x \mid y)\,p(y) / p(x)$, the **prior** $p(y)$ is your belief before seeing $x$; the **likelihood** $p(x \mid y)$ is how well $y$ explains the observation; the **posterior** $p(y \mid x)$ is the updated belief; the **evidence** $p(x)$ normalizes. In short, posterior $\propto$ likelihood $\times$ prior.

**How ML uses it.** In classification the unknown is a label: Naive Bayes and GDA multiply a class-conditional likelihood by the class prior $\phi_k$, and a strong prior can overturn a larger likelihood (the L8 p.37 poll). In estimation the unknown is a parameter: $p(\theta \mid \mathcal{D}) \propto p(\mathcal{D} \mid \theta)\,p(\theta)$, and its maximizer is the MAP estimate. Laplace smoothing is a MAP estimate under a Beta prior.
