**What it is.** Choose a family of distributions $P_\theta$, then pick the parameter under which the observed data were most probable:

$$\hat\theta = \argmax_\theta P_\theta(\mathcal{D}) = \argmax_\theta \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log P_\theta(x^{(i)}) \quad \text{(i.i.d. data)}.$$

The recipe is: write the likelihood, take logs, set the derivative to zero, and check that the critical point is a maximum. For a coin it gives the fraction of heads; for a die, the fraction of each face; for a Gaussian, the sample mean and the $\frac1n$ sample variance.

**How ML uses it.** It turns "fit a model" into "minimize a loss": the negative log-likelihood. Minimizing logistic regression's cross-entropy and least squares under Gaussian noise are maximum-likelihood fits; the Naive Bayes and GDA parameters are maximum-likelihood estimates.
