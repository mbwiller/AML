**What it is.** For two distributions $p$ and $q$ on the same outcomes,

$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \,\|\, q) = \sum_x p(x)\log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)},$$

with the natural log and the convention $0 \log 0 = 0$. It is $\ge 0$, with equality exactly when $p = q$ (Gibbs' inequality), and it is $+\infty$ if $q(x) = 0$ somewhere $p(x) > 0$. It is not a metric: in general $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\,\|\,q) \ne D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\,\|\,p)$, and it can violate the triangle inequality.

**How ML uses it.** It splits as cross-entropy minus entropy, $H(p, q) - H(p)$. With $p$ the empirical distribution of the data and $q = q_\theta$ the model, the entropy term is a constant, so minimizing the KL over $\theta$ is maximum likelihood. The slides write it $D(p|q)$.
