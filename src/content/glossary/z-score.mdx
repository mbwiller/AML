**What it is.** The z-score of a value is its distance from the mean in units of standard deviation:

$$z = \frac{x - \bar x}{s},$$

where $\bar x$ and $s$ are the sample mean and standard deviation of that feature. Standardizing a feature this way gives it mean 0 and standard deviation 1 and removes its units. If $x \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, then $(x - \mu)/\sigma \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$.

**How ML uses it.** Distance-based methods need it: k-means on age in years and income in dollars clusters by income alone until both are z-scored (the point of the L10 p.45 poll; standardization is on p.46). Gradient descent also converges faster on standardized features, because the loss contours become rounder. Compute $\bar x$ and $s$ on the training set only, then apply the same transformation to the test set (sklearn's `StandardScaler`).
