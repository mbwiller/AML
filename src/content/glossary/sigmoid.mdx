**What it is.** $\sigma(z) = \dfrac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$ maps any real $z$ into $(0, 1)$, with $\sigma(0) = \tfrac12$, $\sigma(-z) = 1 - \sigma(z)$, and $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)\,(1 - \sigma(z))$. Its inverse is the logit, or log-odds: if $p = \sigma(z)$, then $z = \log\dfrac{p}{1 - p}$. So $\sigma(z) > \tfrac12$ exactly when $z > 0$.

**How ML uses it.** Logistic regression models $P(y = 1 \mid x) = \sigma(\theta^\top x)$. Any two-class model whose log-odds are affine in $x$ has a sigmoid posterior: Bernoulli Naive Bayes and GDA with a shared covariance both do, which is why their decision boundaries are hyperplanes. With $K$ classes the sigmoid becomes the softmax.
